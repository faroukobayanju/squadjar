# Business model

Planned for mainnet. None of this is live: the testnet app charges nothing, and we have no revenue or users to report.

## Who pays

Members of a squad. No treasurer takes a cut, because there is no treasurer.

## Revenue

1. **Payout fee.** 1% of a payout, capped, taken only when a member collects. It replaces the cut a traditional alajo keeps for holding the money. The fee is shown before the member joins.
2. **Yield share.** Deposits and jars sit locked for the length of a squad. On mainnet that money can earn yield, and Squadjar would keep a share. Members' principal is never put at risk for this.
3. **Group plans.** Associations, cooperatives and unions run many squads with shared reporting, member onboarding and a branded invite page, for a flat plan fee.
4. **Credit partners.** The trust record is built from real payment history. With the member's consent, lenders can use it to offer credit to people with no bank history. Squadjar earns a referral or data-access fee.

## What has to be true first

- Mainnet deployment and a real naira on-ramp and off-ramp.
- The no-deposit hold-back model (issue #27), so entry is cheaper for new members.
- A legal review of fees, yield and credit data in Nigeria.
